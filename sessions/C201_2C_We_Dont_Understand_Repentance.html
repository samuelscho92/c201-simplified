SESSION 2C — WE DON'T UNDERSTAND REPENTANCE

Course 201 · Chapter 2 · Repentance

Key Words

Word Meaning
misconception a wrong idea or belief about something
refreshing a feeling of new life and joy; being made new
ego your sense of pride and self-importance
humility knowing you are not above others; accepting your weakness
contrition deep, genuine sorrow for sin
intercession praying on behalf of others

Introduction

Many Christians have wrong ideas about repentance.

These wrong ideas stop them from repenting freely.

In this session, we will correct four common misconceptions.

Misconception 1: Repentance Is Gloomy

Some people think repentance is always a sad, dark experience.

Repentance is serious. It involves sorrow. But the Bible promises something surprising:

Repentance brings times of refreshing.

Acts 3:19–20 (NLT)

Now repent of your sins and turn to God, so that your sins may be wiped away. Then times of refreshing will come from the presence of the Lord...

Definitions:

Word Meaning
repent¹ to turn away from sin and back to God
wiped away² completely removed; gone forever

When we repent, we are restored to close fellowship with God.

This brings real joy.

Yes, repentance hurts our pride. But pride is harmful to us. When pride dies, something better grows in us — a child-like freedom and peace with God.

Discussion Question: Have you ever said sorry to someone and felt a sense of refreshing in that relationship afterward? Have you experienced this with God?

Misconception 2: Repentance Is Purely Negative

Some people think repentance is only about feeling bad.

But repentance is actually an invitation to freedom.

Quote — Jerry Bridges, Discipline of Grace:

"The gospel, applied to our hearts every day, frees us to be completely honest with ourselves and with God. The assurance of total forgiveness through Christ means we don't have to hide from our sins anymore. We can call sin exactly what it is — because we know that Jesus bore that sin in his body on the cross."

(Simplified from original)

When we repent, we realize the cross of Jesus reaches deeper than we thought.

We understand more of God's grace. Our love for him grows.

Misconception 3: Repentance Should Be Done Rarely

Some people think repentance is costly, so it should be saved for big moments.

But Jesus taught the opposite. He showed this in a beautiful way.

John 13:4–8 (NLT)

So he got up from the table, took off his robe, wrapped a towel around his waist, and poured water into a basin. Then he began to wash the disciples' feet, drying them with the towel he had around him.

When Jesus came to Simon Peter, Peter said to him, "Lord, are you going to wash my feet?"

Jesus replied, "You don't understand now what I am doing, but someday you will."

"No," Peter protested, "you will never ever wash my feet!"

Jesus replied, "Unless I wash you, you won't belong to me."

Definitions:

Word Meaning
protested³ strongly said "no"; refused

Washing feet was the job of the lowest servant. In that time, feet were very dirty from walking on dusty roads.

Jesus used this picture to teach us something important.

Jesus came for one reason: to wash away our sins. His foot-washing was a picture of his death on the cross.

When Peter said "never wash my feet," Jesus answered sharply: "If I do not wash you, you have nothing to do with me." This is literally true. If we keep hiding our dirtiest parts from Jesus, we push him away.

Jesus wants to wash you. That is what he came to do. Do not be shy to bring your worst, most shameful self to Jesus — daily.

Misconception 4: Repentance Should Lead to Instant Change

Some people think: "If I truly repented, I would not do that sin again."

But this is not how repentance works.

The word for repentance in Greek is metanoia. It means "change of mind."

True repentance does change your mind about sin. You hate the sin. You love God who forgives you.

But change in behavior may take time — sometimes a long time.

Example: You have a bad temper. You hurt someone, repent, and promise not to do it again. But then you lose your temper again. This is discouraging. But inner change is happening. Repentance is leading you to humility and dependence on God.

Quote — Roy Hession, Calvary Road:

"To be broken is the beginning of revival. It is painful, it is humbling, but it is the only way."

(Simplified)

Quote — Roy Hession, Calvary Road:

"The Lord Jesus cannot live in us fully until the proud self within us is broken. This means the hard, unyielding self — which justifies itself, wants its own way, stands up for its rights, and seeks its own glory — finally bows its head to God's will. It admits it is wrong. It gives up its own way to Jesus. In other words, it dies to self."

(Simplified from original)

Isaiah 57:15 (NLT)

The high and lofty one who lives in eternity, the Holy One, says this: "I live in the high and holy place with those whose spirits are contrite and humble. I restore the crushed spirit of the humble and revive the courage of those with repentant hearts."

Psalm 51:17 (NLT)

The sacrifice you desire is a broken spirit. You will not reject a broken and repentant heart, O God.

Definitions:

Word Meaning
lofty⁴ very high
contrite⁵ deeply sorry for wrong; broken before God
humble⁶ not proud; knowing your weakness before God

Perhaps the biggest change that repentance brings is not a great triumph over sin. It is something quieter: an enduring spirit of humility and contrition before God and before other people. This is actually a very deep and beautiful change.

Pause and pray: Is God speaking to you about a sin right now? Confess it to him. Then thank him for the gift of repentance through the cross of Jesus.

Part 5: Repent AND Believe

Mark 1:14–15 (NLT)

Later on, after John was arrested, Jesus went into Galilee, where he preached God's Good News. "The time promised by God has come at last!" he announced. "The Kingdom of God is near! Repent of your sins and believe the Good News!"

Notice Jesus said repent AND believe. Not just repent.

Beneath every sin is a deeper sin — a failure to believe the gospel.

Example: You react with anger when someone criticizes your work. On the surface, the sin is anger. But the deeper sin is: you have been finding your worth in your work, not in God. The gospel says you are loved and accepted by God because of Jesus — not because of your work. When you repent, you must also return to trusting that truth.

Repentance is not just stopping a sin. It is returning to the gospel with fresh faith.

Pitfalls of Not Understanding Repentance

If we do not understand repentance properly, we will experience:

  • Defensiveness — We cannot hear truth about ourselves
  • Avoidance of truth — We hide from honest self-examination
  • Distance from God and others — Fellowship weakens
  • No joy or life — We miss the refreshing that repentance brings
  • Viewing God as impossible to please — We live in fear
  • Hypocrisy — We say one thing and live another

Discussion Questions:

  1. Which misconception about repentance have you believed?
  2. Have you experienced "times of refreshing" after repentance?
  3. What does it mean to repent AND believe the gospel?
