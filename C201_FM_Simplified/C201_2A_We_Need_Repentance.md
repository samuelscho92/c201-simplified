# Session 2A: We Need Repentance

*Course 201 · Chapter 2: Repentance · Module 2A*

God wants to be close to us. When we sin, we want to hide from him. Repentance is how we come back to a close relationship with God.

## Key Words for This Session

| Word | Meaning |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Repentance** | To turn away from sin and turn back to God. To stop hiding and tell God the truth about what you did. |
| **Sin** | Anything we think, say, or do that goes against God. |
| **Confess** | To tell God what you did wrong. To stop hiding it. |
| **Fellowship** | Close friendship. Being together with God in an open and honest way. |
| **The Gospel** | The good news that Jesus died on the cross to pay for our sins, so we can be friends with God again. |

## 1\. God Wants to Be Close to Us

Think back to when you were a child. Perhaps you broke something in the house, or you received a bad grade on a test. What did you do? Most of us tried to hide it. We did not want our parents to find out.

This is something all people do. When we do something wrong, we want to hide. We hide what we did. We hide the truth. We hide ourselves.

This problem started long ago, in the Garden of Eden. Adam and Eve sinned against God. After they sinned, they hid from God.

### Genesis 3:8–10 (NLT)

⁸ When the cool evening breezes were blowing, the man and his wife heard the Lord God walking about in the garden. So they hid¹ from the Lord God among the trees. ⁹ Then the Lord God called to the man, "Where are you?" ¹⁰ He replied, "I heard you walking in the garden, so I hid. I was afraid because I was naked²."

**Word Help**

1. **Hid** — Went to a place where God could not see them. Tried to stay away from God.  
2. **Naked** — Not wearing any clothes.

Adam was naked. He was ashamed. He hid from God.

But God did not leave him alone. God came looking for him. God asked, "Where are you?"

Notice something important. God already knew where Adam was. God knew what Adam had done. But God still came to find him. God still wanted to speak with him.

God still asks people this question today. "Where are you? Let us talk. What have you done? Who are you becoming?"

God wants to speak with us. He wants to help us, even when we have made trouble for ourselves.

### Isaiah 1:18 (NLT)

¹⁸ "Come now, let's settle¹ this," says the Lord. "Though your sins are like scarlet², I will make them as white as snow. Though they are red like crimson², I will make them as white as wool."

**Word Help**

1. **Settle** — To talk about a problem and fix it together.  
2. **Scarlet / Crimson** — A deep, dark red color. The Bible uses this color to describe sin.

From the start of the Bible to the end, God's invitation is the same. He says, "Let's talk."

God wants fellowship with us. He wants us to walk closely with him, like a father walks with his child. He does not want anything to hurt this friendship. He wants us to be open and honest with him. He does not want us to hide. He does not want us to be afraid.

Stop and think about that for a moment. The God who made the whole world wants to be close to you. He is not waiting to punish you. He is waiting to speak with you.

### 1 John 1:5–10 (NLT)

⁵ This is the message we heard from Jesus and now declare¹ to you: God is light, and there is no darkness in him at all. ⁶ So we are lying if we say we have fellowship with God but go on living in spiritual darkness²; we are not practicing the truth. ⁷ But if we are living in the light, as God is in the light, then we have fellowship with each other, and the blood of Jesus, his Son, cleanses³ us from all sin. ⁸ If we claim we have no sin, we are only fooling ourselves and not living in the truth. ⁹ But if we confess our sins to him, he is faithful and just⁴ to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all wickedness⁵. ¹⁰ If we claim we have not sinned, we are calling God a liar and showing that his word has no place in our hearts.

**Word Help**

1. **Declare** — To tell people something important.  
2. **Spiritual darkness** — Living in sin. Hiding sin from God.  
3. **Cleanses** — Makes clean. Washes away.  
4. **Faithful and just** — God always keeps his promises. He always does what is right.  
5. **Wickedness** — Sin. The bad things we do.

God wants a close friendship with us. This brings joy. But there is a problem. We like to hide. We hide because we do not want anyone to see our sin.

This is actually good news.

The first words Jesus said when he began to teach were, "Repent and believe the good news."

What is the good news? The good news is this: God is holy. But God is also waiting to forgive you. He wants you to stop hiding. He wants you to stop saying "I did not sin." He wants you to tell him the truth and confess your sin.

That is good news\! That is the gospel\!

### Mark 1:14–15 (NLT)

¹⁴ Later on, after John was arrested, Jesus went into Galilee¹, where he preached² God's Good News. ¹⁵ "The time promised by God has come at last\!" he announced. "The Kingdom of God³ is near\! Repent of your sins and believe the Good News\!"

**Word Help**

1. **Galilee** — A place in Israel where Jesus taught many people.  
2. **Preached** — Taught people about God in public.  
3. **The Kingdom of God** — The place where God is king. The people and the world that follow God.

## 3\. Repentance Brings Us Close to God

All Christians know about repentance. We could not be saved without it. We turned to God by repenting. But this is also true: we live our Christian life every day by repenting.

Repentance is how we have fellowship with God. Without repentance, we cannot have fellowship with God. (Unless we never sin. But 1 John 1:8–10 tells us that all people sin.)

For Christians, our place as God's forgiven child is safe forever. God has called us righteous. Our relationship with God will never end.

Consider your relationship with your mother or father. You are always their child. Some days you may make them happy. Some days you may make them sad. Some days you may even argue with them. But you are still their child. They are still your parent. Nothing can change this.

It is the same with God. All of our sins are paid for by Jesus. This includes our past sins, our sins today, and even our future sins. When we trusted in the work Jesus finished on the cross, God forgave us. God called us righteous. So our sins do not change our place as God's child.

There is a famous saying that helps us understand this:

*"There is nothing you can do to make God love you more. And there is nothing you can do to make God love you less."*

Jesus finished all the work on the cross. God gives us forgiveness as a free gift. This is what grace means.

**But here is the important point: our sins still affect our friendship with God.**

Our place as God's child is safe. But our closeness with God can be hurt by sin.

Consider again your relationship with your parents. You are always their child. But if you lie to them, something feels different. You feel uncomfortable around them. You do not want to look them in the eyes. You are still their child, but the closeness is hurt.

It is the same with God. Adam hid from God after he sinned. In the same way, our sins make us afraid of God. We start to doubt that we are really his child. We become proud. We do not want to admit that we have sinned.

All of this makes our love for God weaker. It makes our friendship with him weaker.

## 4\. A Story: The Broken Bowl

### Part One: The Mistake

Consider this story. Imagine I have a ten-year-old son. He loves to play football. He plays it every day after school.

One day, I sit down with him. I say, "Listen carefully. Do not play football inside the house, especially not in the living room. There is a very special bowl in the living room. It is very old. Your grandfather gave it to me before he died. Please, do not play near it."

My son nods his head. He says, "Yes, Father. I understand."

But the next week, while I am at work, my son plays football in the living room anyway. He kicks the ball hard. The ball hits the bowl. The bowl falls and breaks into many pieces.

My son is afraid. His heart is beating fast. He picks up the broken pieces. He uses glue to put them back together. He puts the bowl back on the table. From far away, the bowl almost looks normal. But if you look closely, you can see the cracks.

Soon, I come home from work. Usually, my son runs to greet me at the door. But today, he does not. The house is quiet. He is in his room.

What will my son do now? There are several ways he might respond. Each one is a real way that people respond when they sin against God.

### Path 1: My Son Hides His Sin

At dinner, my son is very quiet. When I ask about his football team, he looks worried. He watches everything I do. When I start to walk toward the living room, he tries to stop me. He says, "Father, please come play a game with me in my room."

The days go by. I never find out about the broken bowl. But something has changed between us. My son seems far away from me. He does not laugh with me as he did before. He does not want to spend time with me.

His sin is still there, like a wall between us.

*Why does my son act this way? What is he afraid of?*

### Path 2: My Son Tries to Earn My Love Back

My son stays in his room. He works very hard on his homework. He cleans his room without being asked. He does extra chores around the house. He tries to be the perfect son. He thinks, "If I am very good, perhaps my father will not be angry when he finds out."

But here is the problem. He is trying to earn what is already free. My love for him does not come from his good work. My love for him comes because he is my son.

*Why is my son working so hard? What is he hoping will happen?*

### Path 3: My Son Runs Away

My son packs a small bag. He puts in some clothes and some food. He climbs out the window. He leaves a note on his bed. The note says: "I am sorry, Father. I am not good enough to be your son anymore. I am leaving. Please do not look for me."

He thinks he is doing the right thing. He thinks, "My father deserves a better son. I am too bad to come back."

But this is not love. This is shame. He has decided that his sin is bigger than my love for him. He has forgotten that I am his father.

*Why does my son think he must leave? What does he believe about himself, and about his father?*

### Path 4: My Son Becomes Angry

My son closes the door of his room. He turns on loud music. He starts to say things to himself like, "The rules are not fair. Why should I listen to my father? He has too many rules. I will do what I want."

He is angry. But he is not really angry at me. He is angry at himself. It is easier for him to be angry at me than to feel sad about what he did. So in his mind, he makes me the one who is wrong.

*Why is my son acting this way? What is happening inside his heart?*

### The Path I Hope For

This is what I truly hope my son will do. And this is the path the Bible teaches us to take with God.

I come into the living room. I see the broken bowl with the glue marks. I know right away what happened. I am sad about the bowl. But I am more sad that my son is hiding from me.

I want to be with him. I want him to come to me. But he does not come.

So I go to him. I sit down next to him at dinner. I say gently, "Son, after dinner, would you like to go into the living room and look at that special bowl with me?"

My son cannot hold it inside anymore. He starts to cry. He tells me everything. "Father, I am so sorry. I played football in the living room. I broke the bowl. I tried to fix it. I was afraid to tell you."

I pull him close and hug him. I tell him, "Son, I love you. The bowl is only a thing. You are my child. Please do not hide from me when you do something wrong. You can always come to me. I will always forgive you. I will always help you."

Then we sit together and speak. I ask him gently, "Why did you play football in the living room? Did you forget what I said? Did you think it would be acceptable?" We have a real, honest conversation. This conversation helps him grow.

After this, something wonderful happens. We feel closer than before. Our friendship is stronger, not weaker. He learned that his father loves him even when he makes mistakes. And I learned more about my son's heart.

This is what God wants with us. When we sin, he does not want us to hide. He wants us to come to him. And when we come, he is not waiting to punish us. He is waiting to love us.
