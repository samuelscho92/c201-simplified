## Part 1: What the Bible Says About Hell

The Bible is very clear: every person will die and then face judgment.

These verses show what God's word says about eternity:

**Hebrews 9:27 (NLT)**

> Each person is destined to die once and after that comes judgment.

**2 Thessalonians 1:9 (NLT)**

> They will be punished with eternal destruction, forever separated from the Lord and from his glorious power.

**Matthew 25:41 (NLT)**

> Then the King will turn to those on the left and say, "Away with you, you cursed ones, into the eternal fire prepared for the devil and his demons."

**John 5:24 (NLT)**

> I tell you the truth, those who listen to my message and believe in God who sent me have eternal life. They will never be condemned for their sins, but they have already passed from death into life.

*Definitions:*

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| destined¹ | certain to happen |
| condemned² | declared guilty; punished |
| eternal³ | lasting forever |

Hell is the final separation from God. It is being completely removed from the only source of goodness and light in the universe.

**Sin is separation from God.** Hell is that separation continuing forever — without repair.

## Part 2: The Testimony of Jesus

Jesus spoke about heaven and hell more than almost any other topic.

He taught about it in many stories and teachings — the sheep and the goats, the rich man and Lazarus, the Great Banquet, and more.

As Jesus was dying on the cross, he illustrated the truth of heaven one final time.

**Luke 23:39–43 (NLT)**

> One of the criminals hanging beside him scoffed, "So you're the Messiah, are you? Prove it by saving yourself — and us, too, while you're at it!"
>
> But the other criminal protested, "Don't you fear God even when you have been sentenced to die? We deserve to die for our crimes, but this man hasn't done anything wrong." Then he said, "Jesus, remember me when you come into your Kingdom."
>
> And Jesus replied, "I assure you, today you will be with me in paradise."

*Definitions:*

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| scoffed⁴ | made fun of; spoke with disrespect |
| sentenced⁵ | given a punishment by a judge |
| paradise⁶ | heaven; a place of joy and peace with God |

This criminal had no time to do good works. He simply believed. And Jesus said: "Today you will be with me."

**This is why Jesus died and rose again — to make a way for us to be with him forever.**

**John 14:2–3 (NLT)**

> There is more than enough room in my Father's home. If this were not so, would I have told you that I am going to prepare a place for you? When everything is ready, I will come and get you, so that you will always be with me where I am.

## Part 3: Denial

Many people choose not to think about death and eternity.

They tell themselves: "It will probably be OK."

But what is that hope based on? Nothing real.

People say: "There probably is no hell. And if there is, I will probably get into heaven... somehow."

This is simply refusing to face reality.

**Quote — Blaise Pascal, *Pensées*:**

> "Because people cannot cure death, sorrow, and ignorance, they have decided — in order to be happy — not to think about these things."

*(Simplified from original)*

---

**Discussion Question:** What is your response to the fact that people can be happy simply by choosing not to think about death?

## Part 4: Longing for Heaven

Deep inside every person, there is a longing for something more.

Ecclesiastes 3:11 says: "God has planted eternity in the human heart."

We feel this longing in different ways — through music, beauty, nature, or a moment of deep joy.

These things give us a small taste of something we cannot fully explain.

**Quote — C.S. Lewis, *Mere Christianity*:**

> "If I find in myself a desire which no experience in this world can satisfy, the most likely explanation is that I was made for another world."

*(Simplified from original)*

*Example: You hear a beautiful piece of music and feel a deep longing. But the music itself is not what you are longing for — something came through the music. C.S. Lewis calls this "the inconsolable longing" — a desire for heaven that nothing on earth can fully satisfy.*

The Bible describes us as people who are not fully at home in this world.

We were made for more. We were made for God. We were made for heaven.

---

**Discussion Questions:**

1. Have you felt a desire that nothing in this world fully satisfies? When do you feel it most?
2. How does it change your life to know you were made for eternity?
