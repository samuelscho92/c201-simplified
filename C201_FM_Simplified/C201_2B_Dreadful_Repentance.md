# SESSION 2B — DREADFUL REPENTANCE
**Course 201 · Chapter 2 · Repentance**

---

## Key Words

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| pride | thinking too highly of yourself; not wanting to admit you are wrong |
| self-atonement | trying to pay for your own sin through your own effort |
| worldly sorrow | feeling sorry for sin only because of its consequences to you |
| godly sorrow | feeling sorry for sin because you hurt God and others |
| repentance | turning away from sin back to God |
| gospel | the good news that Jesus paid for our sin so we can be forgiven |

---

## Introduction

Many Christians find repentance difficult or even frightening.

Why? There are three main reasons:

1. **Pride**
2. **Self-atonement** (trying to fix things yourself)
3. **Not trusting in God's grace**

---

## Part 1: Pride

When we sin, it hurts our pride to face it honestly.

We made a promise not to sin. We sinned again. We feel deep disappointment with ourselves.

So we avoid dealing with it. We pretend it did not happen.

But pride is harmful. It keeps us away from God. It keeps us away from the truth.

**Discussion Question:** Can you relate to avoiding repentance because of pride?

---

## Part 2: Self-Atonement

Self-atonement means trying to pay for your own sin yourself.

Consider two men who both failed Jesus. One is Judas. The other is Peter.

**Matthew 27:3–5 (NLT)**

> When Judas, who had betrayed him, realized that Jesus had been condemned to die, he was filled with remorse. So he took the thirty pieces of silver back to the leading priests and the elders. "I have sinned," he declared, "for I have betrayed an innocent man." ... Then Judas threw the silver coins down in the Temple and went out and hanged himself.

**Luke 22:60–62 (NLT)**

> But Peter said, "Man, I don't know what you are talking about." And immediately, while he was still speaking, the rooster crowed. At that moment the Lord turned and looked at Peter. Suddenly, the words of the Lord flashed through Peter's mind: "Before the rooster crows tomorrow morning, you will deny three times that you even know me." And Peter left the courtyard, weeping bitterly.

Judas felt sorry for what he did. But his sorrow was about himself. He tried to fix it on his own.

Peter also felt deep pain. But Peter's tears were toward Jesus. He was sorry because he had hurt the one he loved.

---

**Self-atonement has two serious problems:**

**Problem 1: It leaves Jesus out.**
When we sin, we sin against God. We should go to God for forgiveness. Self-atonement ignores God completely.

**Problem 2: It does not work — and it insults Jesus.**
We cannot pay for our own sin. Jesus died on the cross to pay for our sin. If we try to pay for it ourselves, it is as if we are saying Jesus did not need to die.

---

**2 Corinthians 7:9–10 (NLT)**

> Now I am glad I sent it, not because it hurt you, but because the pain caused you to repent and change your ways. It was the kind of sorrow God wants his people to have... For the kind of sorrow God wants us to experience leads us away from sin and results in salvation. There's no regret for that kind of sorrow. But worldly sorrow, which lacks repentance, results in spiritual death.

*Definitions:*

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| repent⁴ | to turn away from sin and back to God |
| sorrow⁵ | deep sadness and regret |
| salvation⁶ | being saved from sin and its consequences |

**Godly sorrow** is directed toward God. It says: "I hurt God. I need his forgiveness."

**Worldly sorrow** stays focused on yourself. It leads to more pain, not healing.

---

**Discussion Question:** Evaluate this story of repentance:

*"I was living in sin while teaching children at church. One day a student said she could not believe in God because all Christians she knew were hypocrites. She looked at me when she said it. I knew she was right. I stopped my sinful lifestyle. I have been faithfully teaching ever since. I hope God is pleased with me."*

What is right about this person's repentance? What is missing?

## Part 3: Not Trusting in God's Grace

A third reason people find repentance hard: they picture God as angry and hard to please.

They think they must show how sorry they are before God will forgive them.

But this misunderstands the gospel.

---

**Quote — Tim Keller, "All of Life is Repentance":**

**Religious repentance is selfish.**
In religion, we are only sorry for sin because of its consequences to us. We want to avoid punishment.

But in the gospel, sin is wrong because it displeases and dishonors God. So gospel repentance is about God, not us.

**Religious repentance is self-righteous.**
In religion, we try to make ourselves suffer enough to deserve forgiveness. But the gospel says Jesus already suffered for our sin. We simply receive his forgiveness as a gift.

**Religious repentance is bitter.**
In religion, every sin is a crisis — because our only hope is our own goodness. But in the gospel, we know Jesus has accepted us. This makes it easier to admit we are wrong.

*(Simplified from original)*

---

**Martin Luther said:**

> "The entire life of a believer is one of repentance."

This is not a sad statement. It means repentance is *how we grow* as Christians.

The more we understand God's grace, the more freely we repent.

And the more we repent, the more amazing God's grace becomes to us.
